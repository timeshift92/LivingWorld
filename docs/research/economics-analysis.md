# Economics & Demography Reverse Engineering Analysis

Date: 2026-07-07

Scope:

- Installed mod: `C:\Games\RimWorld\Mods\Economics-and-Demography`
- Package id: `helldan.economicsdemography`
- Local RimWorld version target: `1.6`

This document studies E&D as a reference for formulas, virtual stockpiles, trade hooks, and patch risks. It must not own Living World population.

## 1. Project Overview

Economics & Demography adds faction-level population, economic production, virtual stockpiles, inflation, trade history, raid costs, settlement collapse/expansion, and RimWar compatibility.

Main subsystems:

- `WorldPopulationManager`: global `WorldComponent`, split into partial files.
- Demography: adults, children, elders, female ratio, birth/death/maturation/aging.
- Economy: global price modifiers, inflation, silver, player asset cache.
- Production: faction archetypes, biome/tech-level production, item caches.
- Trade: virtual trade between non-player factions.
- VirtualStockpile: abstract inventory and silver with real item materialization.
- Harmony patches: pawn generation, raids, trade sessions, map generation, settlement destruction, pawn death/faction changes, market values.
- UI: main tab and trading history gizmos.

Main namespace:

- `EconomicsDemography`

Dependencies:

- RimWorld / Verse
- Harmony
- UnityEngine

## 3. World State Ownership

E&D's source of truth is `WorldPopulationManager`, but its population is aggregate-first:

- `factionPopulation`: adults by faction id.
- `factionChildren`: child pool by faction id.
- `factionElders`: elder pool by faction id.
- `factionFemales`: female adult count by faction id.
- `factionStockpiles`: virtual inventory and silver by faction id.
- `productionProgress`: per-faction item production progress.
- `globalPriceModifiers`: dynamic price modifiers by `ThingDef`.
- `prodLogs`, `saleLogs`, `buyLogs`, `raidLogs`, `stealLogs`, `consumeLogs`: event-like economic history.

Relations:

- Settlements are counted per faction through `Find.WorldObjects.Settlements`.
- Factions are keyed by `Faction.loadID`.
- Pawns are vanilla pawns generated by RimWorld. E&D may bias gender, subtract/add population, and absorb gear, but pawns are not persistent citizens.
- Armies/raids are still vanilla incident/pawn groups; E&D calculates cost and population availability.
- Caravans/trade are influenced by virtual stockpiles and trade session hooks.
- Economy is faction-owned, not settlement-owned.

Living World gap:

- No individual identity.
- No settlement ownership per citizen.
- No household/family history.
- No resource owner below faction stockpile.

## 4. Update Loop

`WorldPopulationManager.WorldComponentTick` is the main scheduler.

Observed cadence:

- First unpaused tick/session init:
  - clears goods caches;
  - recalculates global prices;
  - updates player asset cache;
  - marks session initialized even on error to avoid TPS death.
- Every `updateIntervalHours * 2500` ticks:
  - `RunDailyTasks`.
- Every 900,000 ticks:
  - `RunMonthlyCleanup`.
- Every 3,600,000 ticks:
  - `CollectImperialTaxes`.

`RunDailyTasks`:

- expire ruins;
- maintain stockpile variety;
- `ProcessDailyGrowth`;
- recalculate prices;
- update player asset cache;
- repay raid debt;
- update inflation baseline/homeostasis;
- drift faction population limit modifiers;
- repair old role and mono-gender state;
- mark market values dirty.

`ProcessDailyGrowth`:

- precomputes settlement counts by faction;
- simulates kidnapping/luring/migration-like transfers;
- produces goods by faction;
- consumes daily maintenance;
- applies starvation deaths;
- computes births, child mortality, maturation, aging, elder deaths;
- collapses underpopulated settlements;
- creates ruins;
- handles nomad rebirth and expansion;
- runs virtual trade.

Schedulers/queues:

- No explicit job queue.
- Uses tick modulo scheduling.
- Uses buffers for maturation and aging.
- Uses production progress dictionaries for fractional production.
- Uses monthly production plans to avoid changing production lines every tick.

## 6. World Generation

E&D initializes faction aggregates lazily through `GetPopulation(f)` and stockpile access:

- starting adults based on number of settlements and tech-level base capacity;
- children/elders as fractions of adult count;
- female count based on real race/gender constraints;
- economic archetype assigned globally and by biome/tech/faction;
- starting stockpile generated from tech level, settlement count, and archetype.

World object generation:

- Can destroy/convert settlements into ruins.
- Can create new settlements on expansion or nomad rebirth.
- Uses `TileFinder.RandomSettlementTileFor`.

Generation "из воздуха":

- Starting population is generated as aggregate numbers.
- Starting stock is generated as virtual goods.
- Real things are created later from virtual stockpile.
- No persistent individual citizens are created.

## 7. Population Model

Population is faction-level cohort data:

- adults: `Dictionary<int,int> factionPopulation`;
- children: `Dictionary<int,float> factionChildren`;
- elders: `Dictionary<int,float> factionElders`;
- females: `Dictionary<int,int> factionFemales`;
- maturation and aging buffers.

Features:

- births by male/female pairs or mono-gender fallback;
- child mortality;
- maturation into adults;
- adult aging into elders;
- elder mortality;
- starvation deaths;
- kidnapping/luring between factions;
- vagrant loss;
- settlement collapse and expansion.

Missing for Living World:

- no named citizens;
- no families;
- no per-settlement citizen ownership;
- no profession per person;
- migration is aggregate transfer, not individual movement.

## 8. Economy Model

E&D's economy is the strongest reference.

Virtual stockpile:

- `inventory: Dictionary<string,int>` where key is `ThingDef.defName` plus optional quality suffix.
- `silver`.
- `GetTotalWealth` with per-tick cache.
- `TryConsumeWealth` consumes silver/items based on inflation and military mode.
- `GenerateRealThings` materializes goods for traders/base trade.
- `MaintainVariety` simulates faction-to-faction purchases.

Production:

- Goods are cached by tech level and category: raw resources, manufactured, food.
- `AnalyzeTileForArchetype` uses biome, hilliness, rainfall, temperature, animal density, space/asteroid detection, faction traits.
- Archetypes include Miner, Farmer, Medical, Technician, Tailor, Generalist, Warrior, Chemist, Jeweler, Hunter, Lumberjack, Rancher, Fisherman, Wholesale.
- `ProduceGoodsCategorized` uses labor force, tech multiplier, economy of scale, complexity penalty, inflation, faction trait, market price, stock saturation, monthly production plans, and production progress.

Trade:

- `ProcessVirtualTrade` simulates exports, barter, raids/theft, silver transfer, stock transfer, and logs.
- Permanent enemies steal/raid stock instead of normal trade.

Inflation:

- Total world silver and population establish per-capita baseline.
- Price modifiers are recalculated from total stock, player assets, tech-level demand, and silver.

Weakness for Living World:

- economy is faction-owned, not settlement/household/army-owned;
- production does not consume real local inputs by settlement;
- no explicit workers/professions;
- hidden global price patch may conflict with other economy mods.

## 9. Military Model

E&D does not create permanent armies. It modifies vanilla raid economics:

- raid pawn gear value is counted;
- incident cost is deducted from faction stockpile;
- unpaid cost can become raid debt;
- starving/poor factions can be blocked or weakened;
- deaths and faction changes alter aggregate population;
- gear can be reabsorbed when pawns leave.

This is useful for Living World, but Living World should reserve actual citizens and equipment before raid generation, not infer cost afterward.

## 10. Ecology

No global wildlife ecology model was found. Animal logic appears only indirectly through goods, meat/leather/wool/milk/eggs archetypes and possible animal products. There is no species population, migration, reproduction, grazing, or hunting pressure simulation.

## 11. Persistence

E&D saves through `WorldPopulationManager.ExposeData`:

- faction population/cohorts/females;
- initialized factions and known ids;
- ruins expiration;
- orbital bases;
- faction stockpiles;
- production progress;
- limit modifiers;
- inflation baselines;
- raid debt;
- processed settlements;
- global price modifiers;
- trade/production/consumption logs;
- monthly production plans;
- demographic buffers.

`VirtualStockpile`, `FactionProductionProgress`, and trading log entries implement `IExposable`.

Persistence problems:

- many dictionaries need null repair and migration logic;
- faction keys use `loadID`, which can be fragile across unusual mod changes;
- aggregate population cannot reconstruct individual history.

## 12. Performance

Good:

- heavy simulation runs periodically, not every tick;
- uses caches for goods, wealth, player assets, production plans;
- precomputes settlement counts in daily demography loop;
- uses fractional buffers/progress to avoid spawning objects;
- virtual stockpiles avoid physical item spam.

Bottlenecks:

- global price recalculation scans many `ThingDef` and stockpile entries;
- `IsSafeDef` creates test things and calls stack checks;
- dynamic market value patch is high-frequency if dirty logic fails;
- map generation patches can spawn physical goods/civilians;
- many dictionaries keyed by `ThingDef` or strings can grow large.

## 13. Weaknesses

- Aggregate-first population is incompatible with Living World's identity requirement.
- Faction-level stockpile is too coarse for settlement ownership.
- Broad Harmony patches overlap with Living World's future hooks.
- Dynamic market price patch may surprise other mods and storyteller/wealth calculations.
- Some generation is retrospective: infer raid cost after pawns spawned.
- No deterministic event-sourced history.

## 14. Lessons Learned

Use:

- virtual stockpiles as a concept;
- update cadence and caches;
- tech/biome/terrain-driven production;
- starvation, collapse, expansion, vagrant rebirth ideas;
- trade session boundaries;
- raid cost/debt accounting;
- population cohorts as derived summaries.

Do not use:

- faction aggregate as source of truth;
- direct global market value patch as first implementation;
- generating civilians/loot broadly on map generation;
- reflection-based RimWar integration;
- `Faction.loadID` as the only stable identity for Living World entities.

Rewrite for Living World:

- faction population aggregate -> derived from `WorldCitizen` ownership.
- virtual stockpile -> `OwnedResourceStack` per settlement/army/caravan/faction.
- production -> settlement service using terrain, biome, technology, workers, buildings, and inputs.
- raid cost -> reservation before materialization, outcome after dematerialization.
- trade history -> event-sourced ledger entries.
